What technical skill did you grow most in over the six weeks?

The most significant growth was in transfer learning — specifically understanding
*why* differential learning rates work and what catastrophic forgetting actually
looks like in practice. In Phase 4, when I ran Config B (full fine-tuning with
backbone LR=1e-4), I saw the first-layer Gabor-like edge detectors degrade by
epoch 20 — they literally turned into noise-like patterns. Seeing that visually
made the theory real. Before this project, I treated pretrained weights as a
black box you unfreeze and retrain. Now I understand them as a carefully-learned
feature hierarchy that must be protected.

I also grew substantially in reading and implementing research papers from scratch.
CBAM (Woo et al., ECCV 2018) was the first paper I implemented end-to-end. The
sequential channel-then-spatial design took three attempts to get right because the
shared MLP in channel attention needs careful reshaping. After implementing it,
I could read similar papers (SE-Net, ECA-Net) and understand the design choices
immediately.

Which decision had the biggest impact, positive or negative?

Choosing ResNet50 as the backbone in Phase 3 was the most impactful positive
decision. The jump from 63.88% (MLP) to 92.1% (ResNet50+CBAM) was dramatic, but
the real insight was *why*: 23.5M parameters of hierarchical visual features from
ImageNet, transferred to plant disease detection in minutes. That result — more than
any lecture — convinced me that representation learning is the central idea in
modern deep learning.

The biggest negative decision was attempting DCGAN before VAE for the Potato
Healthy augmentation. With 152 images, the discriminator memorised the training
set within 30 epochs, and training collapsed. I wasted two days debugging GAN
training dynamics before the Phase 4 report literature pointed me to VAE stability
properties on small datasets. The lesson: match the generative model to your data
size, not to what sounds impressive.

Where did you get stuck longest, and what got you unstuck?

The HPO study (Phase 4) stuck me for three days. Optuna kept returning different
"best" configurations on repeated runs even with a fixed seed, because the
MedianPruner was pruning trials based on wall-clock time rather than epoch count.
I eventually traced it to the n_warmup_steps parameter — it needs to be high enough
that early noisy results don't dominate the pruning decision. Setting
n_warmup_steps=8 stabilised the study. The fix was three lines of code, but
finding it required reading the Optuna pruner documentation carefully.

What would you do differently if you were starting over?

I would design the evaluation protocol from day one. In Phase 2, I ran the test
set repeatedly while debugging, which violated the test-set ceremony protocol I
understood only by Phase 5. I would also establish a common `Config` dataclass in
Phase 1 that all notebooks share, rather than refactoring hyperparameters in Phase
4 when the codebase was large.

What surprised you about deep learning that you did not learn from coursework?

The fragility of class-imbalanced evaluation. In Phase 2, the MLP appeared to have
63% accuracy — decent for a baseline. But Potato Healthy F1 was 0.000: the model
had learned to never predict that class, and macro-averaging with a class-zero F1
term masked how broken this was. Macro F1 is not just a metric choice; it is an
ethical choice. If you use accuracy alone on an imbalanced dataset, you hide failure
modes that matter most in deployment.
